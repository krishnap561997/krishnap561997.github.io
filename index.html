Postdoctoral research

ML-based reduced-order models for microchannel flow boiling

ONR-funded · technical lead, computational modeling team

Microchannel flow boiling cools high-performance computing hardware, power electronics and compact evaporators, yet its design still relies largely on empirical correlations and trial and error. The difficulty lies in the physics: bubble nucleation and growth, bubble merger, elongated bubbles and the thin liquid films they leave behind interact nonlinearly across scales, and their behavior depends on many dimensionless groups at once. Our team combines experiments, interface-resolved simulations and physics-informed machine learning to build reduced-order models that span the full sequence of regimes, from heterogeneous nucleate boiling through elongated bubbles to thin films.

Alongside my own work on thin films, I advise students on theoretical modeling and on developing interface-resolved simulations of heterogeneous nucleate boiling and elongated bubble flow, the regimes that precede thin-film formation in the channel.

Thin-film instabilities

Most of the heat transfer in microchannel flow boiling comes from evaporation of the thin liquid film between the vapor and the wall, so predicting how that film behaves is central to predicting performance. In this work, I am developing:

  • Interface-resolved DNS of thin-film flows in Basilisk to capture the nonlinear growth of interfacial instabilities.
  • A reduced-order solver built on analytical formulations that matches DNS accuracy at about 100× lower cost.
  • A machine-learning framework that estimates the ROM's closure terms and extends its accuracy across the parameter space.

A fluid mechanics-informed statistical framework for indoor airborne transmission

Fast risk models such as the Wells–Riley model treat a room as perfectly well-mixed. This ignores the distance between an infected and a susceptible person, and it cannot account for the uncertainties caused by turbulence and inhomogeneity. I instead treated airborne transmission as a coupled turbulent dispersal and statistical problem.

  • Simulations: massively parallel Euler–Lagrange LES in Nek5000 across flow rates, room geometries and ventilation systems, including HVAC filtration and heat sources.
  • Statistical overloading: a novel method that exploits one-way coupling to obtain two-point, two-time Lagrangian statistics across nearly 25 million possible locations, with rigorous uncertainty estimates.
  • Findings: quantified the effects of proximity, air changes per hour and filter efficiency, including a non-zero worst-case ACH for short-term exposure.
  • Model: a data-driven stochastic model that predicts mean dispersal and its turbulence-driven uncertainty.

Collaborations with Pusan National University, UT Rio Grande Valley, AtkinsRéalis and LG Electronics led to 10 journal papers and 11 conference talks.

Related work

Seabed scour around offshore wind farms

2024–2025

I contributed to CFD frameworks for seabed scour around offshore structures and built a low-cost Python/MATLAB tool that evaluates hydrodynamic loading on subsea cables, avoiding up to 11,000 CPU hours of repeated simulations.

RANS vs. LES for indoor flows

with Pusan National University

We replicated the LES framework with RANS in ANSYS Fluent across five room and ventilation configurations to assess where the lower-fidelity approach can be trusted.

Perfusion bioreactor modeling

2021

I performed 3D flow and waste-clearance simulations of bioprinted liver tissue to guide feeding and waste-removal schedules.

Viscoelastic MHD flows
